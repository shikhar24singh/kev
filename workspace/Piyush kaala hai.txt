Anti-Black racism is a systemic form of discrimination rooted in historical oppression, its manifestations persist in modern society, and its societal impact remains profound. Historically, anti-Black racism emerged during the transatlantic slave trade, which institutionalized racial hierarchies and dehumanized Black people. This legacy continued through Jim Crow laws in the U.S., apartheid in South Africa, and colonial systems worldwide, enforcing segregation, violence, and economic subjugation. The civil rights movements of the 20th century challenged these structures, yet systemic inequities persist.

Today, anti-Black racism manifests in police brutality, mass incarceration, and discriminatory practices in education and employment. Black communities face higher rates of poverty, limited access to quality healthcare, and stereotypes that perpetuate bias. Media representation often reinforces harmful narratives, while microaggressions in daily interactions contribute to a climate of marginalization. Movements like Black Lives Matter highlight the ongoing struggle for justice, demanding accountability for violence and systemic reform.

The societal impact of anti-Black racism is far-reaching. It perpetuates economic disparities, limits opportunities, and erodes trust in institutions. Psychologically, it fosters feelings of alienation and low self-worth among Black individuals. Globally, it undermines efforts to achieve racial equality, requiring sustained advocacy, policy changes, and cultural shifts. Addressing anti-Black racism demands collective action to dismantle its roots and build a more just society.
